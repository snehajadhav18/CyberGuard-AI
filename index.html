🚨 Common Cyber Threats to Watch Out For

🎣
Phishing
Fake messages pretending to be banks, companies, or the government to steal your details.
💸
Scams
Too-good-to-be-true offers, fake prizes, or urgent requests for money.
🔓
Weak Passwords
Simple or reused passwords make it easy for hackers to take over your accounts.
🕵️
Social Engineering
Manipulative tactics to trick you into giving away sensitive information.

      💡 Safety Tips

      Practical steps to protect yourself and your family online.

      📧 Email & Messages

      1
      Never click links in unexpected emails. Go directly to the company's official website instead.
      2
      Check the sender's email address carefully. Fake emails often use addresses like "support@paypa1.com".
      3
      Be suspicious of urgent requests. Phrases like "Act now or your account will be closed" are classic scare tactics.
      4
      Never share OTPs. No legitimate company will ever ask for your OTP over a call or message.

      🔑 Passwords & Accounts

      5
      Use a unique password for every account. If one account is hacked, others stay safe.
      6
      Enable two-factor authentication (2FA). This adds a second layer of protection even if your password is stolen.
      7
      Use a password manager. Apps like Bitwarden or 1Password can generate and store strong passwords.
      8
      Never share your password — not even with IT support, bank staff, or close friends.

      🌐 Browsing Safely

      9
      Look for HTTPS before entering personal information — but note that HTTPS alone does not guarantee a site is safe.
      10
      Avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive activities like banking. Use a VPN if you must use public networks.
      11
      Keep your software updated. Updates fix security holes that hackers exploit.
      12
      Be careful what you download. Malicious software often hides in free downloads or pirated content.

      📱 Social Media & Privacy

      13
      Review your privacy settings regularly. Limit who can see your posts, location, and personal details.
      14
      Do not overshare online. Your date of birth, address, and phone number can be used to impersonate you.
      15
      Be cautious of friend requests from strangers. Fake profiles are used to gather information or run scams.
      16
      Think before you post. Once something is online, it can be very difficult to remove permanently.

      🏦 OTP & Banking Safety

      17
      Never share your OTP with anyone. Not bank employees, not tech support, not family. Your bank will never ask for it.
      18
      Hang up on suspicious callers. If someone calls claiming to be from your bank and asks for your PIN or OTP, it is fraud.
      19
      Set transaction limits on your bank account. Lower daily limits reduce losses if your account is compromised.
      20
      Monitor bank statements regularly. Report any unauthorized transaction immediately.

      📶 Public Wi-Fi Safety

      21
      Avoid banking or shopping on public Wi-Fi. Cafes and airports often have unsecured networks.
      22
      Watch out for fake Wi-Fi hotspots. Criminals set up hotspots to intercept your data.
      23
      Use a VPN on public networks. A VPN encrypts your connection.
      24
      Turn off auto-connect. Disable auto-connect to known networks on your phone.

      💻 Device Security

      25
      Keep all software updated. Security patches fix vulnerabilities attackers exploit.
      26
      Use a strong screen lock. Set a PIN, password, or biometric lock on your devices.
      27
      Install apps only from official stores. Avoid side-loaded APK files.
      28
      Back up your data regularly. Backups protect you from ransomware and hardware failure.

      🔐 Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

      29
      Enable 2FA on every important account. Even if your password is stolen, attackers cannot log in without the second factor.
      30
      Prefer an authenticator app over SMS. Apps like Google Authenticator are more secure than SMS codes.
      31
      Store your 2FA backup codes safely. Print and lock them away — never store them in your email.
      32
      Never share 2FA codes with anyone. No legitimate service will ever ask you to read your 2FA code aloud.
